Lucid Dreaming Guide

A few habits that make it more likely you'll notice you're dreaming — while you're still in it. Your Dream Journal above is the single best tool for this: the more you reread your dreams, the more their recurring signs jump out at you while you're dreaming.

Reality checks

🖐 Hands & fingers
Look at your hands. In dreams, fingers often look wrong, blurry, or the wrong count.
🔢 Re-read text
Read a sentence, look away, read it again. Text usually changes or turns to nonsense in a dream.
💡 Light switches
Flip a switch. Lighting rarely responds correctly in dreams.
👃 Nose-pinch breath
Pinch your nose shut and try to breathe through it. If you still can, you're dreaming.
❓ Question wakefulness
Ask "am I dreaming?" for real, several times a day. The habit carries into your dreams.

Keep a dream journal (you're already doing it)

Write your dream down the moment you wake, before you move or check your phone for anything else. Capture fragments and feelings, not just what "happened" — and watch for symbols that keep coming back. Every dream you summon above is saved to your Journal, so this habit is already built in.

MILD — Mnemonic Induction of Lucid Dreams

  1. As you fall asleep, set a clear intention: "next time I'm dreaming, I'll remember I'm dreaming."
  2. Replay a recent dream from your Journal in your mind, and imagine yourself noticing you're dreaming inside it.
  3. If you wake from a dream in the night, do a reality check before falling back asleep — that's often the moment lucidity clicks.

WBTB — Wake Back To Bed

  1. Wake up roughly 4.5–6 hours after falling asleep (an alarm works).
  2. Stay awake briefly — about 20–30 minutes — doing something low-stimulation, like reading or reviewing a Journal entry.
  3. Go back to sleep holding your lucidity intention. Combined with MILD, this is when lucid dreams are most likely.
